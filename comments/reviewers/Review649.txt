Reviewer 2 of ROMAN 2026 submission 362

Comments to the author
======================

The paper proposes PoseOFF, a pose-anchored optical flow
representation for low-latency human action anticipation in
human-robot teaming. The main idea is to extract local
optical flow features around human joints and combine them
with skeleton-based representations. The paper is well
motivated, and the proposed method is clearly positioned
between efficient skeleton-based methods and
computationally expensive dense optical-flow-based
approaches. The experimental results on NTU RGB+D 60, NTU
RGB+D 120, and UCF101 show consistent improvements over the
baseline models, especially under partial observation
settings. Overall, I find the paper interesting and
relevant to the conference. I have the following comments
for the authors to consider.

1. The motivation of the proposed PoseOFF representation is
generally clear. However, the authors may further clarify
the difference between PoseOFF and existing joint-centered
or pose-guided motion representations. This would help
readers better understand the novelty of the proposed
method beyond simply combining pose and optical flow.

2. The experimental results are promising, especially the
improvements in early action anticipation. Nevertheless, it
would be helpful to provide more discussion on why the
gains are larger on UCF101 than on the NTU datasets. Since
UCF101 contains more unconstrained videos and estimated
poses, further explanation would strengthen the
interpretation of the results.

3. The paper reports the computational cost and inference
time of PoseOFF, which is useful. However, it would be
beneficial to clarify whether the reported inference time
includes optical flow estimation or only the model
inference after feature extraction. Since optical flow
computation can be expensive in practice, this distinction
is important for evaluating real-time applicability.

4. The quality of several figures is rather low. In
particular, the curves, legends, and small text in some
figures are difficult to read, and some visual examples are
almost unclear in the current version. The authors should
improve the resolution, enlarge the fonts, and ensure that
all figures remain readable after being compressed into the
conference format.

5. Some minor writing and formatting issues should be
corrected. For example, there appear to be a few
typographical errors and awkward expressions in the text,
such as “datsets” and some broken or unclear sentences. A
careful proofreading would improve the overall presentation
quality.
